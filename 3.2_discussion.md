## Discussion Prompt

As you have seen, lists of comparable companies can be built specific to GICS subindustries, by topic similarity with the LDA algorithm, or by similarity in semantic meaning with the Doc2Vec algorithm. While these methods each have their own unique strengths, there is a significant difference in the correlations drawn between companies as a result of their alternative approaches to the development of comparable lists.

Given your experience at this point, do you feel that there are areas where one method is preferable to another in the construction and management of an investment portfolio? While GICS is still maintained as an industry standard against which comparable company lists are built to this day, there are restrictions to those classifications that limit their applicability. However, while LDA and Doc2Vec may offer improvements over GICS in the relevance of correlations drawn between companies, their respective efficacy is limited by the relevance of input data.

How might you determine which method to employ when building a list of comparable companies for the construction and management of an investment portfolio?

- Areas of strong alignment between Doc2Vec/LDA and GICS.
- Areas where Doc2Vec is superior.
- Areas where LDA is superior.
- Areas where GICS is superior.
- Which method would you choose given your own investment interests?

## My Response

I’d choose the method based on the actual portfolio task rather than treating one as universally best. If Doc2Vec/LDA and GICS all point to the same peer set, that is a strong signal that the firms are comparable both economically and linguistically. In that case, I’d trust the overlap most.

Doc2Vec is stronger when the goal is to capture business-model similarity that standard industry labels miss. It is especially useful for firms that sit across multiple segments or use language that reflects strategic shifts earlier than GICS updates. LDA is helpful when I want interpretable topic structure and a middle ground between rigid classification and fully dense embeddings.

GICS is still better when consistency, transparency, and explainability matter most. For portfolio construction, that matters a lot because sector exposure, benchmarking, and risk reporting still rely on standardized industry buckets.

For my own investing, I’d use a hybrid: start with GICS for structure and risk control, then use Doc2Vec to refine the comparable set, with LDA as a check on whether the similarity is driven by coherent business themes.
