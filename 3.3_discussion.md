## Discussion Prompt

Thematic ETFs are in some ways a perfect demonstration of the power of algorithms like Doc2Vec and LDA to replicate or even construct a diverse list of companies across industry sectors based on their relevance to a desired theme. While this process can be iterative in nature, the ability to refine inputs and customize outputs specific to your needs provides a significant advantage over more subjective methods for construction.

In the case of Doc2Vec, semantic vectors can be developed and refined specific to keywords of your choice, providing company associations that may not have been readily apparent through traditional means. However, your first attempt at replicating the ARKG ETF focus yielded an overlap of just 28.4%, which shows that your semantic vectors captured some, but not all, of the companies present in that fund.

While a portion of that difference was a result of the limited dataset employed (Top 1000 domestic companies), there were still a number of companies missed that might have been incorporated had you further refined semantic vectors. Take a moment and share your thoughts on the topic with your classmates, structuring your response around the following question:

How would you adjust your approach to semantic vectors to create a list of stocks more reflective of the ARKG ETF theme?

- What words would you add/remove from your list of semantic vectors?
- How deep of a list of companies would you provide for comparison? (You evaluated the top 25 companies, whereas the ARKG ETF has 43)
- How would you weight your list of semantic vectors? (The ARKG ETF has significant weight in the Exact Sciences, favoring companies like Beam)

## My Response

I’d refine the vector list by making it more biologically specific and less broadly “innovation” focused. I’d add terms like `genomics`, `gene editing`, `CRISPR`, `precision medicine`, `mRNA`, `cell therapy`, `molecular diagnostics`, and `synthetic biology`. I’d probably remove or downweight vague words like `disruption`, `innovation`, or `technology`, since those can pull in firms that sound growth-oriented but are not really aligned with ARKG’s theme.

I also would not stop at the top 25 names. Since ARKG holds 43 companies, I’d want at least a top 50 or even top 60 list for comparison, then evaluate precision within that broader set. That gives more room for smaller but thematically relevant firms to appear.

For weighting, I’d overweight terms tied directly to genomic medicine and diagnostics, and underweight adjacent healthcare language. That should better capture firms like Beam or Exact Sciences, where the science is central rather than just loosely related.
